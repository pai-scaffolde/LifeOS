## Gap 1. Team, partners, data access and approvals

**Why it matters.** The request asks for a team to start working, but the overview named no human roles (for example a computational modeller, a fly geneticist and two-photon imager, a real-time rig engineer, a bioacoustics and machine-learning lead, and a welfare and ethics lead). It also named no fly facility, rigs, stock budget or partner labs, although it notes that most identity-resolved courtship results come from three labs (T04). R10's 90-day plan includes building a finch call rig. A finch is a vertebrate, so the work needs institutional approval, and T09 covers only insect welfare law. Several week-1 items depended on access nobody had verified: the Pacheco 2021 data and BIFROST templates ("access unconfirmed", T04), and public paired song-plus-pose courtship data ("availability unverified", T06; T09 lists the Murthy- and Stern-lab data locations at [LOW]). The answers decide what is realistic in 90 days, whether to partner or build, and whether R10's finch work must wait for an approval step.

**Data access.** The Pacheco et al. 2021 whole-brain auditory dataset is public on Princeton DataSpace (DOI 10.34770/gv6w-5351), and its readme lists no licence restrictions. It holds across-trial median auditory responses from 33 wild-type flies, plus a second set from 5 wild-type flies that adds natural song [HIGH · T10][1]. This resolves T04's "access unconfirmed" for Pacheco. The deposit holds medians, not raw volumes, so a volumetric reanalysis would still need the authors. The stimulus list for the 33-fly set (pulse, sine and white noise) matches the paper but was not in the readme snippet [1].

BIFROST (Brezovec et al., PNAS 2024) has a public Dryad deposit and is also distributed as a Snakemake workflow. It builds an in vivo Functional Drosophila Atlas and registers data to it with under 10 µm precision, validated on genetically labelled cell types [MED · T10][2]. Whether the Dryad deposit includes the atlas template files is unconfirmed. The skeptic removed a statement that the software uses SynthMorph, which no result mentioned. It also notes that the first author is indexed as Brezovec BE, not LE [2].

Courtship song audio is public. The Stern-lab deposit for Stern et al. 2017 (PNAS) on Dryad totals 6.75 GB: a 3.50 GB recordings archive and a 3.26 GB segmentations archive, posted 25 August 2018 [HIGH · T10][3]. "Audio only, no pose tracking" is inferred from the archive names and the microphone-only rig, not stated. Arthur et al. (BMC Biology 2013) describe a recording system that captures 32 courting pairs at once, with open-source song segmentation software. They used it to analyse more than 18 hours of song from 75 males of five wild-type strains [MED · T10][4]. Only the software is stated to be open source. The paper describes the hardware but does not release it as an open design, and the 2013 hardware may need updating.

Pose data exist separately. SLEAP's public flies13 dataset holds labelled pose frames of freely interacting virgin male-female pairs, with a 13-node skeleton and identity labels, and no audio [MED · T10][5]. The researcher's eight searches found no public dataset with song and pose recorded together, and no Calhoun 2019 deposit. That negative is a report of the searches and cannot be verified. The skeptic found a Murthy-lab courtship-behaviour dataset on Princeton DataSpace (doi 10.34770/me8y-8k48; CC-BY 4.0; collected at Princeton 2015-2018) whose contents, including whether it pairs song with tracking, are unchecked [6]. T06's "availability unverified" therefore stands, now with a concrete lead to check before asking the Murthy or Stern labs for a data-sharing agreement.

**Approvals.** Under the Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act 1986 as amended by SI 2012/3039, a procedure on a protected animal for a scientific or educational purpose is a regulated procedure if it may cause pain, suffering, distress or lasting harm equal to or greater than that caused by the introduction of a needle in accordance with good veterinary practice. A regulated procedure needs a project licence, a personal licence and a licensed establishment [MED · T10][7]. Whether finch song playback alone crosses that threshold depends on the protocol, for example isolation, deprivation or implants. In the UK the classification is settled with the establishment's Named Persons and the Home Office, with the AWERB advising. In the US, an approved IACUC protocol is normally needed for any vertebrate use under PHS Policy, whatever the severity [MED · T10][7]. The skeptic could not confirm the regulation number or the URL path; legislation.gov.uk usually uses /regulation/ for statutory instruments [7]. No source found says whether finch playback alone is regulated in the UK, so R10's finch rig waits until the institution's animal ethics committee has classified the protocol (T10).

**Rigs and partners.** Published tethered-courtship imaging gives two precedents. A male fly tethered for two-photon imaging can court a virtual moving "female" in closed loop, and the gain of LC10a visual projection neurons rises selectively during courtship, tied to arousal and P1 activity [MED · T10][8]. This is the Ruta-lab study that T03 and T04 already cite for LC10a gain. In tethered males with a wild-type fruitless background, moving light spots evoke courtship-like following with one-sided wing extension only after optogenetic priming of dsx-expressing pC1 or pC2l neurons. Calcium imaging of pC1 showed periodic rises, each matching one of the male's turns [MED · T10][9]. The behaviour is following and wing extension, not full courtship, and the preparation needs transgenic flies (a dsx driver plus an opsin). A Tohoku University summary describes the priming as pheromone-related, so the exact protocol should be read in the Methods [9]. No search found component lists or costs for either rig, and staffing and a fly facility were not searched; both remain open. The Ruta lab as a partner for the real-time rig is a planning suggestion (T10).

## Gap 2. Measuring internal state directly: neuromodulators, neuropeptides and male mating drive

**Why it matters.** Receiver and sender state is the program's central variable: the Short answer, R1, R3 and R5's state-aware decoding all depend on it. Yet the plan's only state inputs were mating status and states inferred by GLM-HMMs. The dossiers name the missing pieces without reviewing them. T01 lists dopamine, mating drive, satiety and age as not reviewed or audited; T04 says GRAB sensors are not covered; T05 notes that the transmitter classifier the models depend on has no neuropeptide class. A measured state channel would add a state-readout arm to R1 and R3 imaging, a male-drive covariate to R2 and R5, and peptidergic edges to R4 and R7.

**Male mating drive.** In male Drosophila, a recurrent excitatory loop between pCd and NPF neurons raises a dopamine signal that increases courtship. Loop activity builds with abstinence and, through the activity-dependent transcription factor CREB2, drives production of potassium channels that suppress activity. Each mating lowers loop activity, and the authors' model shows it recovering over about 3 days [MED · T10][10]. The neuron counts were not visible in any result. The loop was imaged and manipulated in the lab, not measured in freely courting flies, so using time since last mating as a covariate for drive is a program inference (T10).

As courtship nears copulation, dopamine signalling through Dop2R receptors inhibits the visual threat-detection pathway, so males stop aborting courtship when threatened. Earlier in courtship, threat-activated visual neurons act on central courtship nodes through serotonergic neurons [MED · T10][11]. The source is an abstract mirror. A statement that brain dopamine rises with courtship progress came only from press coverage that was not retrieved, so it is not kept. The paper shows dopamine gating causally but, as far as these results go, gives no dopamine-level readout.

Opposing dopaminergic and GABAergic neurons control how long a male persists in copulation. Just after mating begins he endures even life-threatening stimuli to continue, but about 15 minutes later mild stimuli make him end the mating. Dopaminergic neurons support high motivation and GABAergic neurons reduce it [MED · T10][12]. The source is a lab profile summarising Crickmore and Vosshall (Cell 2013); the primary paper was not checked, so the timing should be confirmed there. The method of recovering motivation from stimulus tolerance could be reused as a behavioural state readout (T10).

**Sensors.** Next-generation GRAB dopamine sensors, including the red rGRAB_DA1m and rGRAB_DA1h, detect compartment-specific dopamine release from a single neuron in live flies. They can be co-expressed with GCaMP6s to record dopamine and neuronal calcium together [HIGH · T10][13]. The snippet does not say which variant gave the single-neuron result. GRAB_OA1.0, built on the fly Octβ2R receptor, is highly specific for octopamine and has sub-second kinetics, with a peak fluorescence increase of about 660% per the source snippet. In vivo it reported mushroom-body octopamine release during odour and shock, and that release depended on Kenyon-cell acetylcholine acting through nicotinic receptors [MED · T10][14]. The 660% figure was not in the skeptic's own search results. No result showed either sensor used during courtship or mating, and GRAB_OA1.0 has not been tested in female-receptivity circuits. A state-readout arm therefore has to be built and validated rather than borrowed (T10).

**Peptidergic maps.** About 80 neurosecretory cells of the adult brain were reconstructed in the FlyWire female connectome. They have few classical presynapses, consistent with paracrine neuropeptide release. The authors combined the connectome with single-cell transcriptomics to infer neuropeptide receptor expression and putative paracrine connections [MED · T10][15]. The source is the eLife reviewed-preprint reviews page, and the author list is unconfirmed. A FlyWire-based connectome of the circadian clock integrated single-cell transcriptomics and receptor mapping to infer paracrine peptidergic signalling, which significantly enriched interconnectivity within the clock network [HIGH · T10][16]. Its count of about 240 clock-network neurons, against the 150 previously assumed, matches the published abstract as recalled but was not in the snippet. Both studies give a worked method for adding peptidergic edges to FlyWire, so far for single subnetworks. No whole-brain peptidergic connectome was found. Edges added to R4 or R7 this way are inferred links, not measured ones (T10).

**Female satiety.** In starved females, octopaminergic AkhR neurons suppress receptivity. They act on Octβ1R-expressing LH421 neurons, which inhibit the sex-promoting pC1 neurons through GABA and the Rdl receptor. Knocking down Octβ1R in LH421 abolishes the starvation-induced suppression [HIGH · T10][17]. In this paper's account pC1 also integrates courtship stimulation and mating status, so the female's state as a receiver includes satiety as well as mating status. The paper is Sun et al., "A neural pathway underlying hunger modulation of sexual receptivity in Drosophila females", Cell Reports 42(10):113243, 2023, reached here through a DOAJ index record [17]. "Virgin" was not stated in the retrieved text.

## Gap 3. Indicator kinetics and fast readouts for timing claims

**Why it matters.** R2's computational first step is to measure how far pIP10 calcium leads song onset in the Shiozaki 2024 data, so that a later phase can respond to intent. R1 depends on vpoDN latency to single song bouts. No dossier covers indicator kinetics. T04 asks whether jGCaMP8 or voltage indicators are needed, and T01 notes that the pIP10 signal pools left and right cells and that no voltage imaging of song neurons exists. If indicator rise and decay times are of the same order as the tens-to-hundreds of ms lead being sought, a calcium-based lead estimate can be biased in either direction. The answer changes R2's analysis (model the indicator, or wait for faster sensors) and sets the latency budget for closed loop, where T04 notes that pulse-level closed loop has not been shown.

**Calcium indicator kinetics.** The jGCaMP8 paper (Zhang et al., Nature 2023) reports half-rise times as short as 2 ms. A Janelia news release says the figure holds only in some applications, so it is a best case that depends on variant and preparation [MED · T10][18]. The same paper states that, after single action potentials in mouse pyramidal neurons, most widely used GCaMPs have half-rise times on the order of 100 ms [MED · T10][18]. R2's assumed lead of tens to hundreds of ms is a project assumption, not from this source. If it holds, an undeconvolved calcium lead would be confounded by rise kinetics of the same order (T10).

In field-stimulated cultured neurons, half-decay times are about 182 ms for jGCaMP7f, 67 ms for jGCaMP8f, 118 ms for jGCaMP8m and 307 ms for jGCaMP8s. One variant, probably jGCaMP8f, is described as having 4x faster rise and 2.5x faster decay than jGCaMP7f [MED · T10][19]. These are cultured rodent neurons, so the values rank the variants but do not give in vivo kinetics in fly neurons. At the Drosophila larval neuromuscular junction, under 40 Hz motor-axon stimulation, all jGCaMP8 variants rose faster than jGCaMP7f, and jGCaMP8s appears to decay slightly more slowly than jGCaMP7f [MED · T10][20]. The source is a patent, and reading its "the latter" as decay is an inference. Presynaptic boutons under 40 Hz trains are not adult central neurons, and train-evoked rise is not single-event onset latency. The result suggests, but does not show, that jGCaMP8f or 8m would sharpen onset timing in song neurons. No search found in vivo indicator kinetics in the adult fly central brain.

**What the Shiozaki data hold.** Shiozaki et al. 2024 recorded calcium in the nerve cord of singing males. One population is active during both sine and pulse song, and an expanded population that includes it is active during pulse song, so the additional neurons are recruited for pulse. Brain recordings showed the same nested pattern in two descending pathways required for singing, which a Janelia release calls the "when" and "what" pathways [MED · T10][21]. The search results did not name pIP10 as one of the pathways and did not give the indicator variant, the frame rate or any onset-lag analysis; these must be read from the paper's Methods. A statement that the companion methods follow Shiozaki et al. 2022 with GCaMP under fru-Gal4 was not confirmed.

**Voltage imaging in flies.** Light-field microscopy imaged nearly the whole adult Drosophila brain at up to 200 Hz with a 20x objective, using GCaMP or the voltage probe ArcLight [HIGH · T10][22]. The skeptic recalled, but did not confirm by search, that the flies could walk or groom during imaging and that ArcLight showed previously unreported spontaneous activity. Population voltage imaging in an adult fly is therefore feasible. A 200 Hz frame rate does not give 5 ms voltage resolution, though: ArcLight has slow components of tens of ms, and light-field reconstruction blurs space. The method is not single-neuron and was not applied to song neurons. The chemigenetic indicator Voltron was used for in vivo voltage imaging in mice, zebrafish and flies, including a pair of dopaminergic neurons, one in each hemisphere, in adult flies [MED · T10][23]. Spike detection confirmed by electrophysiology is reported for mouse cortex; whether the fly recordings were spike-resolved needs the full text. Voltron also needs delivery of a HaloTag dye ligand, a practical hurdle in a singing-fly preparation. Neither indicator has been used on song neurons or singing flies.

**Deconvolution.** On ground-truth datasets with simultaneous electrophysiology, simple non-negative deconvolution beat the other spike-inference algorithms compared on out-of-sample data, and a pure decaying-exponential kernel did better than kernels with a rising phase [MED · T10][24]. The ground truth was mouse data, mostly GCaMP6. The benchmark scores spike-train correlation, not onset timing, so it does not show that deconvolution removes rise-time lag from a lead estimate (T10). A further statement that per-cell tuning of the kernel timescale hurt performance was not in the snippet and was not verified, so it is not kept.

**Dropped in review:** none. One uncited lead remains: a Huang lab (Washington University) page on chronic pAce voltage imaging in behaving flies, undated and unverified.

## Gap 4. The full courtship signal repertoire: wing displays, female copulation song and aggression song

**Why it matters.** The overview modelled the male as sending three song modes plus vibration, and the female as replying by slowing, vaginal plate opening, ovipositor extrusion or stopping. A contingent partner (R1, R2) that classifies turns this way will mislabel other wing behaviours and miss the female's own acoustic signal. T01 found a 2026 Current Biology title, "Sequencing of distinct wing behaviors during Drosophila courtship", without its content. T01 and T06 report female copulation song at [HIGH], with effects on remating and male success, but nothing on its neurons beyond DsxF, and the overview omitted it. T01 also reports that the aggression-song neurons are unknown. Checking 2025-2026 work decides whether R2's mode classifier and R1's reply set need more classes, and whether R3 has a natural female acoustic signal to inject.

**Waggling, a silent wing display.** The title T01 found is a paper by Li, Thieringer, Gao and Murthy: a bioRxiv preprint from August 2025 that a Princeton listing reports as in press at Current Biology [25]. Males perform a non-acoustic courtship wing behaviour called waggling. Both wings are extended, at smaller angles than in song, which uses one wing, and oscillate rapidly in anti-phase at a median of about 10.6 Hz. The microphones detect no sound from it [HIGH · T10][25]. The 10.6 Hz figure appeared in the snippet but not in the skeptic's own search summary.

Courtship follows a directional three-step motif: male and female both slow to near-stillness, the male begins waggling, and he then moves into courtship song. Wing movements during a waggle bout predict which wing he uses in the songs that follow [HIGH · T10][25]. The authors suggest that waggling may be preparatory; that function is not demonstrated. Optogenetic activation of specific P1/pC1 subsets in solitary males, with no female cues, reproduces the whole stillness-to-waggling-to-singing sequence [MED · T10][25]. This shows that driving the circuit is sufficient to generate the sequence. It does not show that natural sequences start without the female. Waggling also occurs in D. simulans and D. sechellia, and in D. sechellia it uses wider wing angles and slower oscillations [MED · T10][25]. Frequency and wing-angle limits for this class should therefore not be reused unchanged across species (T10). An audio-only classifier of male modes will miss waggling, so R2's classifier needs video-based classes for waggling and for the stillness before it (T10).

**Female copulation song.** Females produce a song by wing vibration during copulation. It differs acoustically from male courtship song, requires neurons that express DoublesexF, and depends on components of seminal fluid transferred from the male accessory gland. Under sperm competition, males that hear it have higher reproductive success, which suggests that female acoustic cues may shape how males allocate ejaculate [MED · T10][26]. The skeptic removed the descriptions "high-pitched chitter" and "pulsed", which no source showed. T01 audited the same paper at [HIGH · T01] and narrowed "depends on seminal fluid" to "modulated by", the paper's title wording; the program uses T01's wording outside this addendum. Two searches found no 2025-2026 work on the circuit beyond DsxF. That negative was not checked by the skeptic, and a von Philipsborn lab project page describes ongoing work on the seminal-fluid trigger, not on the circuit. The song follows acceptance, so it is a natural female acoustic signal but not a pre-mating turn (T06).

**Aggression sound and the threat display.** Male aggression sound lacks the sine-like component of courtship song and is usually produced with both wings moving together. Its inter-pulse intervals are about twice as long as in courtship song and more variable [MED · T10][27]. The venue and year (Fly, 2011) come from a repository summary, not the journal. Silencing the AB sound-sensitive Johnston's organ neurons, or disrupting their signal-transduction genes, reduces male-male aggressive encounters. Playback of agonistic sounds increases aggression, and courtship-song playback reduces it [MED · T10][28]. The repository record did not show authors or year; T01 cites the same title as Versteven et al. 2017, PNAS [HIGH · T01]. No 2025-2026 work identified the neurons that produce aggression sound.

The visual threat display coordinates wing and leg actions (wing elevation, pumping, turning and charging). It needs a cluster of about 3 AIP neurons that are not required for other aggressive behaviours (Duistermars et al. 2018). Connectome analysis in a 2025 Anderson-lab bioRxiv preprint finds that AIP neurons project, in a feed-forward arrangement, directly to the descending neurons DNpe050 and DNp60 and to the interneurons AVLP491 and AVLP710m. Activating DNpe050 and DNp60 together drives robust wing movement, and the preprint also reports that activating DNpe050 alone raises the probability of wing elevation [MED · T10][29]. The split of AIP targets into a wing module and a leg module rests on the connectome and a truncated thesis abstract, and the leg-module details were not visible. The display was not shown to be silent. The evidence is wiring plus activation in a preprint that has not been peer reviewed. A Drosophila "scissoring" display could not be confirmed in any source.
